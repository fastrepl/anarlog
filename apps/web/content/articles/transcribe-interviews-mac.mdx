## How do you transcribe a live Zoom, Meet, Teams, or phone interview on Mac without a bot?

OS-level audio capture records the remote speaker from macOS system output and your voice from your microphone at the same time, so it works identically across Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack huddles, browser interviews, and Mac Continuity phone calls without adding a guest bot to the call.

Here is the step-by-step setup in **Anarlog** (native Rust + GPUI `2.0` on macOS 15 or later):

1. **Install Anarlog and verify audio permissions.** Download the Apple Silicon or Intel Mac DMG from [Anarlog](/download/) and move it to Applications. On first launch, grant **Microphone** and **System Audio** capture permissions in macOS System Settings.
2. **Choose your speech-to-text (Transcription) path.** Open **Settings -> Transcription**. On **Apple Silicon Macs**, select **Soniqo Parakeet Streaming** if you want text appearing live while you interview, or **Soniqo Parakeet Batch** if you want the highest local accuracy and speaker labels processed right after the call. You can also load a local `whisper.cpp` `.bin` model, use Apple Speech (macOS 26+), plug in your own API key (Deepgram, OpenAI, etc.), or use Pro cloud STT. On **Intel Macs**, configure a local Deepgram-compatible server, BYOK, or Pro cloud.
3. **Choose your summary (Intelligence) path separately.** Open **Settings -> Intelligence** and select **Ollama**, **LM Studio**, **Unsloth**, or eligible **Apple Intelligence** if interview notes must stay strictly on your Mac. Or use BYOK (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google Gemini, Mistral, OpenRouter) with an approved enterprise key, or Anarlog Pro hosted AI. See our [offline Mac transcription setup guide](/blog/offline-meeting-transcription-mac/) for air-gapped verification steps.
4. **Take brief anchor notes during the interview.** Start recording at the beginning of the interview. Instead of transcribing every word manually, write short cues in the Anarlog editor: candidate concerns, exact quote markers, follow-up prompts, or product reactions.
5. **Apply an interview template and export.** When you stop recording, Anarlog pairs your anchor notes with the local transcript to build a structured summary while preserving the full transcript and audio file in local SQLite (`app.db`). Export to Markdown when you are ready to move quotes into an article draft, UX repository, or ATS scorecard.

Keep one architectural boundary in mind: Anarlog Pro adds encrypted Cloud Sync across unlimited desktop and mobile devices plus hosted AI, whereas signing out and selecting local STT plus local Ollama or LM Studio keeps interview processing on your Mac.

## How do you transcribe recorded interview files on Mac (MP3, M4A, WAV)?

When an interview is already saved as an MP3, M4A, WAV, MP4, or MOV file on your Mac, pick between a dedicated file transcriber (**MacWhisper**) and importing into your meeting archive (**Anarlog**).

1. **MacWhisper for dedicated batch file transcription:** Open MacWhisper, drag your audio or video files into the window, and select a local Whisper model. The Free edition handles single files with smaller local models and exports TXT, SRT, and VTT. Upgrading to **MacWhisper Pro (€64 one-time)** unlocks larger Whisper models, multi-file batch queues, automatic speaker recognition, and advanced document exports. Local models stay offline unless you explicitly turn on cloud translation or cloud AI prompts.
2. **Anarlog audio import for unified interview search:** If you record in-person interviews on a phone or portable recorder and also run remote interviews on your Mac, import the audio file into Anarlog. Anarlog transcribes the file using your selected local or BYOK provider, lets you generate a structured interview summary with custom templates, and stores the session in local SQLite alongside your live-call interviews so AI chat and search work across your entire research project.
3. **Apple Voice Memos and Notes for quick single recordings:** On Apple Silicon Macs running supported macOS releases, Voice Memos generates a built-in transcript you can copy into another document, and Notes records audio directly inside a note with searchable transcription. Both are convenient for a single quick memo, though they lack multi-file batch export, custom AI templates, and cross-platform conference call routing.

## How do you get clean speaker labels and accurate quotes in Mac interviews?

Transcript errors in Mac interviews usually come from acoustic bleed and Bluetooth headset profiles, not from the speech model itself. Fix the audio chain before tuning prompts:

- **Wear headphones on every remote call.** If the interviewee's voice plays out of your MacBook speakers, your microphone picks it up milliseconds later. That echo confuses speaker diarization and duplicates words in the transcript.
- **Avoid macOS Bluetooth HFP mic fallback.** Standard Bluetooth headphones drop into narrowband 16 kHz hands-free profile (HFP) when their built-in microphone is active, degrading audio clarity. Use wired headphones, or route output to your headphones while selecting your MacBook Pro mic or a dedicated USB microphone as the macOS input device.
- **Run a 20-second dual-channel test before high-stakes interviews.** Record yourself speaking while playing a short voice clip through Zoom, Meet, or QuickTime. Play the file back inside your transcription app to confirm both voices are crisp and balanced before the guest joins.
- **Verify exact quotes, names, and numbers against audio.** Every automated speech model can misspell proper nouns, product acronyms, or dollar amounts. Keep the local audio file attached to the session so you can click and verify a verbatim quote in seconds before publishing or sharing hiring feedback.
- **Use human verification when precision is contractual.** If a transcript requires certified or human-verified verbatim wording for legal or sensitive investigative reporting, upload the exported audio to a human review service such as Rev (starting at $1.99/minute).

## What consent and privacy rules should you check before recording an interview?

Using a bot-free Mac recorder removes the clutter of an extra participant tile, not the legal or ethical obligation to inform the people you interview.

- **Know your recording-consent jurisdiction.** Some US states and countries permit one-party consent, while all-party (two-party) jurisdictions require every participant to know and consent to recording. Because remote interviewees may sit in a different state or country than you, defaulting to explicit verbal disclosure is the safest practice.
- **Follow GDPR and workplace notice rules in Europe.** For recruiting, UX research, or commercial interviews involving EU residents, tell interviewees before recording starts why you are recording, how the transcript will be used, and how long it will be retained.
- **Watch where free cloud tools route interview audio.** Uploading confidential source interviews, executive briefings, or candidate evaluations to a consumer cloud web app may retain recordings on vendor servers or allow model training unless opted out. Storing sessions in local SQLite with on-device transcription (or an enterprise zero-retention API key) keeps data paths predictable and auditable.
