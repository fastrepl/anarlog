**Meetily is the stronger starting point when you need to choose a local transcription and summary stack. Otter is the stronger starting point when you want a hosted workspace for shared meeting history.** Verify the edition and settings in either case: Meetily offers remote providers, and Otter now offers desktop capture as well as meeting-joining workflows.

We make Anarlog. We checked official documentation on September 22, 2026, and tested one synthetic file in Meetily. Otter's illustration is vendor-provided; we did not run a head-to-head accuracy or speed test.

## Compare the workflow you will actually use

| Requirement | Meetily | Otter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Process a meeting without internet after setup | Select downloaded local transcription and summary models | Hosted processing; not an offline transcription choice |
| Choose your own model service | Local, hosted, and configurable provider options | Managed service with the controls of your plan |
| Keep a shared cloud meeting history | Evaluate deployment, exports, and integration requirements | A central part of the product |
| Record without a visible meeting bot | Desktop system-audio capture | Desktop options exist; not every Otter workflow requires a bot |
| Automate access | Pro developer interfaces with scoped access | API, integrations, and MCP availability vary by feature and plan |

Meetily's [current documentation](https://docs.meetily.ai/) is the starting point for its provider choices and desktop workflow. Otter's [desktop page](https://otter.ai/desktop) and [plan comparison](https://otter.ai/pricing) describe its capture options and service limits. Bot-free is a capture method, not a promise of local inference.

## Follow audio and transcript separately

In Meetily, inspect the transcription provider first and the summary provider second. Sending audio to a remote transcription endpoint and summarizing the result locally is still a partly remote workflow. Local transcription followed by a cloud summary sends a different artifact: the transcript text.

Use Meetily's [transcription settings](https://docs.meetily.ai/features/transcription-providers) and [summary settings](https://docs.meetily.ai/features/summary-providers) to check those paths. Do not infer them from the product's general privacy positioning.

Otter maintains a hosted service for transcripts, playback, collaboration, and AI features. Its [security documentation](https://otter.ai/privacy-security) explains storage and access practices. That architecture may be exactly what a distributed team wants: a common meeting record that does not depend on one person's laptop staying available.

## A useful comparison test

Try one synthetic meeting in both products using the capture mode you expect to use every day. Include a name that is easy to mishear, an explicit decision, a proposal that is rejected, and a task without an assigned owner.

Review five things before comparing the polish of the summaries:

1. Did the tool capture both sides of the conversation?
2. Can you trace a summary claim back to the transcript or recording?
3. Did it invent an owner or deadline?
4. Can the intended colleague access the result without an accidental broad share?
5. Can you export the information you need in a usable form?

Repeat the test with authorized audio that includes your usual background noise, vocabulary, and languages.

## Plans change the comparison

Meetily Community and Pro are different editions. Its [MCP documentation](https://docs.meetily.ai/developers/mcp) requires Pro, an enabled local gateway, and scoped credentials. Check the current [pricing page](https://meetily.ai/pricing) for the edition containing a feature before calling it free.

Otter Basic currently lists 300 monthly transcription minutes, a 30-minute conversation cap, and three lifetime file imports. These are separate limits. Paid plans change the limits and add features; the [pricing table](https://otter.ai/pricing) is the source to check before a migration. "Unlimited" in one row does not override a cap in another.

Training policy also varies. Otter's [Enterprise controls](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/13352505516695-Enterprise-Admin-Controls-Overview) document a no-training default for Enterprise workspaces. Do not apply a general consumer-plan description to every enterprise customer.

## Platforms and exports before you migrate

| Edition | Desktop requirements in official sources | Export entitlement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meetily Community | Native macOS/Windows; Apple Silicon Mac download; Linux source build | Basic copy sharing; advanced file formats are advertised for Pro |
| Meetily Pro | Same native desktop availability | Markdown/DOCX/PDF summaries; TXT/DOCX transcripts; clipboard copy |
| Otter Basic | macOS 12.3+ or Windows 10+; web also available | TXT transcript and MP3 audio |
| Otter Pro | Same desktop requirements | Adds DOCX, PDF, and SRT |
| Otter Business / Enterprise | Same desktop requirements | Adds bulk export; shared content still requires export permission |

Sources: Meetily [downloads](https://meetily.ai/downloads), [pricing](https://meetily.ai/pricing), and [export guide](https://docs.meetily.ai/features/export-and-copy); Otter [desktop requirements](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/35973988280215-Otter-Desktop-App-Mac-Windows) and [conversation exports](https://help.otter.ai/hc/en-us/articles/360047733634-Export-conversations).

Meetily Pro's GUI exports one meeting at a time. Its transcript formats preserve timestamps and speaker labels when present. Otter's export controls also expose speaker/timestamp options. Confirm which options you selected before treating an exported file as a complete archive.

Open the output in the destination you actually use. Check a name, a decision, a speaker change, and the last segment of the meeting. Then decide who will maintain the archive and its backups. Complete these checks before switching.

## Which one fits?

Choose Meetily when local processing or a configurable AI stack is non-negotiable and the supported desktop edition meets your needs. Choose Otter when shared cloud history, cross-device access, and managed collaboration are the work you want the product to do.
