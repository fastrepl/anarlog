Microsoft Teams has native AI note-taking, but there is no single feature called the Teams AI notetaker. Microsoft splits the job across Facilitator, Intelligent Recap, and Copilot.

Third-party tools work differently. Some join as a visible participant. Some capture Teams audio from your desktop. Others only work when you join through a browser.

That architecture matters more than another summary-quality score. It determines whether the tool works in external meetings, where the transcript lives, and whether your administrator can block it.

For most people, the shortlist is:

- **Microsoft Facilitator and Intelligent Recap** for organizations already governed through Microsoft 365.
- **Anarlog** for an open-source, bot-free workflow with a local SQLite meeting record.
- **Granola** for a polished bot-free notepad with a hosted team workspace.
- **Tactiq** for live transcription in Teams on the web.
- **Otter** for auto-join and unattended capture.
- **Fireflies** for CRM routing and workflow automation.

## Does Microsoft Teams have an AI notetaker?

Yes, but the answer depends on what you mean by notes.

**Facilitator** generates collaborative notes during a scheduled Teams meeting. It can summarize decisions and open questions, and track an agenda. Task-management features are in public preview. A Microsoft Copilot license is required to turn it on. Other internal participants can see its live updates in Chat and Notes without holding their own Copilot license. [Microsoft documents Facilitator and its current limits here](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/copilot/facilitator-in-microsoft-teams-meetings).

**Intelligent Recap** produces post-meeting notes, suggested tasks, timeline markers, topics, and chapters from a recorded or transcribed meeting. Microsoft currently makes it available through Teams Premium or a Microsoft Copilot license, though individual recap features have different prerequisites. [The current recap guide lists those requirements](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/recap-in-microsoft-teams).

**Copilot** answers questions and generates notes during a meeting. Teams also has an "Only during the meeting" mode that works without creating a persistent recording or transcript. The tradeoff is that Copilot will not remain available in Recap afterward unless someone enables recording or transcription. Purview may still retain prompts and responses under the organization's policy. [Microsoft explains the distinction here](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/copilot/use-copilot-without-transcribing-or-recording-a-teams-meeting-or-call).

![Microsoft Teams Intelligent Recap and Copilot view with AI summary, transcript, and speaker timeline](https://ijoptyyjrfqwaqhyxkxj.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog/library/products/microsoft-teams/recap/2026-07-17-recap.png)

*Microsoft's Recap example keeps the recording, transcript, AI summary, and timeline in one Teams surface. [View the current Microsoft guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/recap-in-microsoft-teams).*

The native option is the logical baseline. Do not pay for another workspace until you know which constraint Teams fails to meet.

## How to turn on Facilitator in Teams

For a scheduled meeting, open its **Options → Copilot and other AI**, allow Copilot and Facilitator during the meeting or during and afterward, enable **Facilitator**, and apply. In an active meeting, use **More actions → Turn on Facilitator**.

If the option is missing, check your Copilot license, organizer/presenter role, and whether you are using Teams web or desktop. Facilitator cannot be added to channel meetings, instant meetings, or Teams calls. Enable transcription if you need to prompt it after the meeting ends. These steps follow [Microsoft's Facilitator guide](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/copilot/facilitator-in-microsoft-teams-meetings), checked September 16, 2026.

If your organization does not enable the native feature, compare an approved alternative below. For Anarlog, check the [offline setup and model requirements](/blog/local-ai-meeting-notes/) before assuming that desktop capture also means local AI processing.

## 1. Microsoft Facilitator, Intelligent Recap, and Copilot

**Best for:** Organizations that want meeting notes governed through Microsoft 365.

Microsoft's advantage is administrative continuity. The meeting, transcript, recap, permissions, and retention controls remain inside the tenant your IT team already manages.

The storage model is more distributed than the Teams UI suggests. Recordings live in the organizer's OneDrive, or the channel SharePoint site for channel meetings. Transcript copies and AI-generated recap artifacts can live in OneDrive, Exchange, or participant mailboxes depending on the feature. Facilitator notes are stored in an editable Microsoft Loop page. [Microsoft's Intelligent Recap privacy documentation maps the current storage paths](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/privacy/intelligent-recap).

### Strengths

- No third-party participant or calendar bot
- Shared real-time Facilitator notes inside Teams
- Recap, transcript, recording, tasks, and meeting content in Microsoft 365
- Existing sensitivity, retention, access, and eDiscovery controls
- Useful across desktop, web, and mobile, with feature-specific limits

### Tradeoffs

- Facilitator requires a Copilot license
- Intelligent Recap requires Teams Premium or Copilot, with exceptions by feature
- Post-meeting AI access normally depends on recording or transcription
- External participants do not get the same access as internal attendees
- Notes remain tied to the Microsoft tenant and its retention policy

Microsoft changed parts of its Teams license packaging on April 1, 2026, and grandfathered some existing Teams Premium entitlements until renewal. Check the current [Teams enhanced-experience licensing table](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/enhanced-teams-experience) rather than copying a plan matrix from an older comparison.

Choose Microsoft when your company already treats Teams as the meeting system of record.

## 3. Granola

**Best for:** Teams users who want a polished, collaborative AI notepad without a meeting bot.

![Granola notepad beside a video call with background transcription active](https://ijoptyyjrfqwaqhyxkxj.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/blog/library/products/granola/notepad/2026-07-19-meeting-note.jpg)

[Granola](https://www.granola.ai/) runs on your computer and uses device audio instead of joining the meeting. Its Teams-specific setup currently requires the Teams desktop app, with Granola and Teams using the same default audio devices. [Granola documents that setup here](https://www.granola.ai/blog/granola-microsoft-teams-bot-free-notes).

Granola is an important comparison because "bot-free" no longer means "local". It removes the guest participant, but the notes and team memory live in Granola's hosted workspace. Granola says it sends audio to a transcription provider during the meeting and does not retain that audio after transcription.

### Strengths

- No meeting participant
- Collaborative notes and searchable team memory
- Available on macOS and Windows, with mobile apps
- Private-by-default notes and enterprise administration on higher plans
- Free plan for ongoing individual use, with a 30-day history limit

### Tradeoffs

- Teams capture depends on the desktop app and audio-device setup
- Meeting history lives in a vendor workspace
- No local database or bring-your-own AI stack
- Enterprise retention and identity controls require the highest plan
- It cannot attend a meeting when you are absent

Choose Granola when you want bot-free capture and shared cloud memory, but do not need local ownership or model choice.

## 5. Otter

**Best for:** Teams that want automatic joining, unattended capture, and a searchable meeting workspace.

[Otter](https://otter.ai/integration/microsoft-teams) can connect to a Microsoft calendar and send its Notetaker into scheduled Teams meetings. The guest participant may need admission under the host's lobby policy. It can keep working when you do not attend, which local recorders and browser extensions cannot do.

Otter is no longer bot-only. Its current desktop apps can capture meeting audio without adding a participant, and it also offers Chrome and mobile capture. That gives an individual a bot-free option while preserving Otter's hosted workspace and AI-agent features.

## Why Teams meeting bots may start failing more often

A calendar integration does not guarantee admission.

Microsoft is rolling out an admin policy that detects potential external bots, places them in the lobby, flags them for the organizer, and requires explicit approval even when other attendees can bypass the lobby. Its separate Bot Identification Program is still in public preview, so do not assume every notetaker is labeled consistently yet. [Microsoft documents the rollout and identification limits here](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/teams-bot-identification).

That changes the tradeoff for external calls. A bot may work in meetings your company hosts but stall in the lobby when a customer or partner owns the tenant. Test both cases before standardizing on auto-join.

## A practical deployment checklist

Before standardizing any Teams notetaker, test the complete meeting lifecycle:

1. Decide which meetings may be recorded or transcribed.
2. Test internal meetings and meetings hosted by another tenant.
3. Confirm whether the workflow adds a participant, uses a browser, or captures desktop audio.
4. Record where audio, transcripts, notes, prompts, and tasks are stored.
5. Check what remains available after the meeting ends.
6. Test deletion, export, retention, and access controls.
7. Decide where approved decisions and tasks move after the recap.

Teams notifies participants when its native recording or transcription starts. Administrators can also require explicit recording consent before attendees can unmute or share video. Third-party desktop capture has a different notification path, so an organization still needs a clear recording policy. [Microsoft documents the explicit-consent control here](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/participant-agreement-recording-transcription).

Recording and consent requirements vary by jurisdiction and meeting context. Review the practical issues in our guide to [AI meeting-note legality and consent](/blog/is-ai-notetaking-legal), then confirm the policy with qualified counsel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Microsoft Teams have a built-in AI notetaker?

Yes. Facilitator creates collaborative live notes, Intelligent Recap generates post-meeting summaries and tasks, and Copilot answers questions during and after supported meetings. Licensing and transcription requirements differ across the three.

### Can Copilot take notes without recording a Teams meeting?

Yes, in "Only during the meeting" mode. Copilot can generate notes and tasks without a persistent recording or transcript. It will not remain available in Recap afterward unless recording or transcription is enabled, and Purview may still retain prompts and responses.

### Can I take AI meeting notes in Teams without a bot?

Yes. Microsoft native features do not add an external bot. Anarlog and Granola capture desktop audio, Tactiq operates in Teams on the web, and Otter and Fireflies offer bot-free desktop modes alongside their meeting participants.

### Which options work with the Teams desktop app?

Microsoft's native features, Anarlog, Granola, and desktop capture modes from Otter or Fireflies work with Teams desktop. Tactiq requires Teams in Chrome, Edge, or the Teams PWA.

### Is a bot-free notetaker automatically private?

No. "Bot-free" only describes capture. Granola, Tactiq, Otter, and Fireflies can avoid a meeting participant while still storing notes in a vendor cloud. Check processing, storage, retention, AI-provider, and sharing paths separately.

### Where does Teams store recordings and transcripts?

Standard meeting recordings go to the organizer's OneDrive. Channel meeting recordings go to the channel's SharePoint site. Transcripts and AI-generated artifacts can also be stored in Exchange or participant mailboxes depending on the feature and policy.
