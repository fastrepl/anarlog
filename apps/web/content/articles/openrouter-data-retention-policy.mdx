OpenRouter is an aggregator that routes your API requests to dozens of underlying providers, including OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral, Google, and many others.

That leaves two retention questions: what OpenRouter stores and what the receiving provider stores.

Freshness review: October 6, 2026.

## What OpenRouter Itself Keeps From Your Requests

According to [OpenRouter's data-collection guide](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/privacy/data-collection), it does not store prompts or responses unless you opt into content logging or data sharing. It does store request metadata, including token counts and latency, for reporting and model ranking.

No prompt storage does not mean no processing. OpenRouter also describes anonymous prompt categorization using a zero-retention model. Its [privacy policy](https://openrouter.ai/privacy) separately covers uploaded files and security or legal exceptions; do not apply the inference default to every feature.

## Two Logging Settings That Do Different Jobs

OpenRouter now distinguishes private logging from permission to use your data. [Its logging documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/input-output-logging) describes both:

- **Private Input & Output Logging**, enabled in Observability, saves prompts and completions for your review. OpenRouter says it does not use this content for training or analytics. Retention is at least three months and can be longer unless you request deletion. Organization admins can view all logged content; members can view requests attributed to them.
- **OpenRouter Use of Inputs/Outputs**, enabled in Privacy, permits product-improvement use in exchange for a 1% usage discount. This is independent of private logging. Both settings are off by default.

For confidential meeting text, keep both off unless the logging requirement and retention have been approved. Private debugging logs still create another stored copy. Do not confuse them with the separate data-discount arrangement.

## What OpenRouter ZDR Actually Enforces

[Zero Data Retention enforcement](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr) filters the underlying model endpoints eligible to receive your request. It is not a switch that merely disables OpenRouter's own prompt logging.

You can enforce it in account privacy settings, through guardrails, or per request with `provider.zdr: true`. Account and guardrail enforcement cannot be disabled by setting the request value to false. Current account settings also support separate model-group scopes.

ZDR and no-training controls answer different questions. A provider can exclude data from training while retaining it for abuse monitoring. Check the exact endpoint rather than inferring its policy from the model name.

Two boundaries matter: OpenRouter treats in-memory prompt caching as compatible with ZDR, and ZDR filtering does not cover every plugin or third-party tool backend. Review those paths separately if you enable them.

## What OpenRouter's Underlying Provider Stores

![An OpenRouter request passes through the gateway, then the underlying provider's own retention policy applies](https://static.anarlog.so/blog/articles/openrouter-data-retention-policy/openrouter-request-path.png)

When OpenRouter routes your request, the receiving endpoint has its own data policy. [OpenRouter documents endpoint-specific policies](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr), including special provider agreements that can differ from a provider's direct API baseline. Do not assume every OpenAI or Anthropic route has the same retention period.

For direct API context, see our [OpenAI retention guide](/blog/chatgpt-data-retention-policy/) and [Anthropic retention guide](/blog/anthropic-data-retention-policy/). Those baselines are not proof of the policy for a particular OpenRouter endpoint.

OpenRouter displays the data retention policy for each provider endpoint in its interface. Before routing sensitive data through a model, check the provider policy shown for that endpoint.

By default, OpenRouter load-balances across available providers, prioritizing price. A preferred provider is not an exclusive provider: [routing documentation](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/routing/provider-selection) explains that fallbacks are enabled by default. Use an allowed-provider list or explicitly disable fallbacks when other providers must not receive the request.

## How to Keep Your Data in the EU

[In-region routing](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/in-region-routing) is available on Business and Enterprise plans for the EU and US. Choose the region by sending requests to `https://eu.openrouter.ai` or `https://us.openrouter.ai`; there is no separate activation setting.

Shared endpoints must be eligible for the selected region, and requests fail rather than fall back out of region. Some features are unavailable on regional hosts. If you bring your own upstream provider credentials, verify that your own cloud deployment is in the matching region too; a regional OpenRouter hostname does not reconfigure infrastructure you own.

## Is OpenRouter Safe for Regulated Industries?

ZDR is a retention control, not a compliance agreement. Before sending protected health information, confirm the applicable BAA, covered services, and every processor in the route with the vendors and your compliance team. Do not infer HIPAA eligibility from a privacy toggle.

For compliance-sensitive deployments, the multi-provider nature of OpenRouter adds complexity. You are not just evaluating OpenRouter's compliance posture but also the compliance posture of every provider your requests might be routed to. Unless you lock routing to a specific provider with known compliance certifications, the compliance surface is difficult to bound.

## Evaluate a Specific Route

For sensitive work, record the exact model endpoint, allowed providers and fallbacks, ZDR enforcement, both logging settings, and any enabled tools. Add regional routing and the relevant contracts where required. A no-training label alone does not establish zero retention.
