**6 Best Read AI Alternatives in 2026**

In January 2026, a Microsoft MVP and Cloud Solutions Architect at Crayon published a breakdown of how Read AI had spread through a client's Microsoft 365 tenant without IT knowing. One employee signed up via a "Sign in with Microsoft" OAuth prompt. Read AI got calendar access, started auto-joining meetings, and emailed summaries to everyone on the invite list, including external clients. Recipients clicked the link, authenticated, and Read AI got access to their calendars too. She called it *"a case study in viral Shadow IT"* in [the post](https://agderinthe.cloud/2026/01/19/read-ai-the-meeting-assistant-you-didnt-invite/). IT teams across [Microsoft's own forums](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/4419230/read-ai) started asking how to block it after discovering it had been propagating through their tenants for weeks.

Read AI has real strengths. The [action item tracking is the most praised feature across 400+ reviews](https://tooliverse.ai/tools/read-ai), and the cross-channel Search Copilot that ties together meetings, email, and Slack is something most competitors haven't touched. Engagement scoring gives managers data they can act on. For organizations with centralized IT that have vetted it, Read AI covers a lot of ground.

But the bot joining every call as a visible participant, recording people who never consented, is what drives most of the churn. Read AI recently [shipped a botless integration for Google Meet](https://www.read.ai/post/read-it-whats-new-at-read-ai----march-2026), which tells you they know the problem. On Zoom and Teams, the bot is still the default. A [free tier capped at 5 meetings per month](https://www.read.ai/plans-pricing), Pro at $19.75/user/month, and a [G2 rating sitting around 3.7](https://www.g2.com/compare/read-vs-sembly-ai-sembly-ai) (unusually low for this category) round out the picture. Four of the six tools below are completely bot-free. The remaining two fill gaps the bot-free options haven't closed yet.

## Best Read AI Alternatives in 2026

### Char

[Char](https://char.com) is open-source and approaches the problem differently from everything else here. It captures system audio without joining your call or requesting calendar permissions. There's no OAuth grant that can propagate through your organization. Everything is stored as plain markdown files on your device, which means your notes are readable by any tool you already use: Obsidian, VS Code, Notion, a plain text editor.

You can take notes during a meeting in Char's built-in notepad while it transcribes in the background, and the AI combines both into structured output after the call. Or you can skip the manual notes entirely and let it generate summaries from the transcript alone. The AI stack is yours to pick: Char's managed cloud service, your own API keys from OpenAI, Anthropic, or Deepgram, or fully local models through Ollama. That last option means the entire pipeline can run offline, on your machine, with nothing leaving your network. For companies that blocked Read AI over data residency, Char is one of the few tools where "local-first" means exactly what it says, because you can read the source code to verify it.

It runs on macOS and Linux. Windows support isn't here yet, and there's no mobile app, no built-in CRM sync, no engagement scoring. Char focuses on transcription, notes, and AI summaries with complete data ownership. Free for unlimited local transcription or BYOK setups. Pro is $25/month for managed cloud. 45+ languages.

### Jamie

Where Char gives you raw ownership and flexibility, [Jamie](https://meetjamie.ai/) packages the bot-free experience into a more conventional product. It's a desktop app for Mac and Windows (plus an iOS app) that captures audio from your device and generates summaries with speaker recognition, action items, and custom templates. The feature set is the closest match to Read AI among bot-free tools: HubSpot and Salesforce integrations, 100+ languages with automatic detection, in-person meeting support, and an "Ask Jamie" semantic search that lets you query across all your recorded meetings.

The privacy credentials are built around European compliance: EU-hosted infrastructure, GDPR compliant, ISO 27001 certified. Audio gets deleted after transcription and customer data is never used for model training. For companies in Europe where Read AI's US-based data handling was the dealbreaker, Jamie was designed for that exact use case. The tradeoff against Char is that Jamie is closed-source and stores data on their servers (EU servers, but still theirs).

[Pro runs €47/month](https://www.meetjamie.ai/pricing) for unlimited meetings. The free plan gives you 10 meetings with a 30-minute limit per session. There's no live transcription during the call (summaries arrive after) and CRM integrations require the higher tiers.

### Tactiq

Char and Jamie are desktop apps. [Tactiq](https://tactiq.com/) lives entirely in the browser as a Chrome extension, which changes the dynamic. You install it, join a call in your browser, and it transcribes in real time with [over a million users and a 4.8-star rating](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/tactiq-chatgpt-meeting-su/fggkaccpbmombhnjkjokndojfgagejfb) on the Chrome Web Store. No bot joins the meeting. The transcript shows up live as people speak, so you can highlight and tag moments during the call rather than reviewing everything after.

The workflow automations are where Tactiq earns its keep. One-click summaries, action item extraction, follow-up email drafting, and reusable prompt templates that push to Slack, Notion, HubSpot, Pipedrive, and Linear. Security is stronger than you'd expect from a browser extension: SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, ISO 27001, HIPAA. No audio is recorded or stored.

[Pro starts at $8/month](https://tactiq.io/buy) (annual) with unlimited transcripts, which makes it the cheapest option on this list by a wide margin. Free gives 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits per month. The limitation is that it only works in the browser. If your team uses native desktop apps for Zoom or Teams, Tactiq can't capture those calls. No in-person meeting support either, which is where Char and Jamie have the edge.

### Granola

Granola shares the same core mechanic as Char's notepad mode: you type rough notes during the call while it captures audio in the background, and the AI merges both into structured output when the meeting ends. The difference is that Granola leans harder into the notepad-as-interface idea. It's designed to feel like you're just writing in a doc, and the AI enhances what you wrote rather than generating a separate summary. For people who want to stay engaged by typing rather than passively letting a tool record, that workflow clicks.

The company raised $125 million in March 2026 at a $1.5 billion valuation. That same month, they encrypted their local database, which broke every agent workflow that had been reading notes from the local cache. [Guido Appenzeller of a16z posted about it on X](https://x.com/appenz/status/1904284791949095032) and it went viral. Granola said MCP access was available; developers wanted a proper API. One has since been announced. It's the kind of incident that illustrates why Char stores notes as plain markdown rather than a proprietary format: you can't encrypt what you don't control.

Speaker identification is weak in multi-person calls and there's no audio playback or CRM integration. The free plan covers 25 meetings lifetime (not monthly). Business is $14/user/month. Available on Mac, Windows, and iOS.

### Fathom

Fathom is only bot-free on Zoom, where it runs as a native plugin invisible to other participants. On Google Meet and Teams, a Fathom bot still joins the call. If your team lives on Zoom, the experience is clean. If you split across platforms, you're back to the same friction that comes with Read AI, just on fewer calls. Worth knowing before you commit.

The free tier is genuinely generous: unlimited recordings, unlimited transcription, no credit card. Advanced AI summaries are capped at 5 per month on free (down from unlimited in 2025), but that still outpaces Read AI's 5-meeting cap on the entire free plan. "Ask Fathom" lets you search across recorded meetings, competing directly with Read AI's Search Copilot. Bot-free recording for Meet and Teams has been announced but hasn't shipped.

CRM sync with Salesforce and HubSpot, custom vocabulary, and coaching analytics sit behind the $19/month paywall, roughly equivalent to Read AI Pro. Fathom stays focused on meetings only, with no cross-channel search or engagement scoring. For Zoom-heavy individuals, it's the lowest-friction starting point, but the platform limitation is real.

### Fireflies.ai

Fireflies uses a bot. I'm including it because the bot-free tools above still have gaps in CRM automation, and for sales teams doing 20+ external calls a week, that gap matters. Fireflies records meetings, generates searchable transcripts, and pushes summaries, action items, and call metadata directly into Salesforce, HubSpot, or Zoho. Topic detection auto-tags segments as "pricing discussion" or "feature request" without configuration. The conversation analytics (talk-time ratios, sentiment, question frequency) go deeper than what Read AI's engagement scores provide.

There's a legal issue worth weighing. Fireflies is facing a [BIPA class action in Illinois](https://www.classaction.org/news/fireflies-ai-collects-meeting-participants-biometric-data-without-consent-class-action-alleges) for allegedly collecting voiceprints from participants who never created an account or agreed to any terms. If your organization blocked Read AI over consent issues, this falls into the same category.

The free tier gives you 800 minutes of storage total (not monthly), which disappears in a couple of weeks of regular use. Pro runs $18/user/month. Jamie and Tactiq now cover CRM sync without a bot, though their integrations aren't as deep as what Fireflies offers on the Salesforce side. For teams where the CRM pipeline justifies the bot tradeoff, the math can work. For everyone else, the bot-free options have caught up enough.

## Which Read AI Alternative Is Right for You?

Read AI's cross-channel Search Copilot, engagement scoring, and Digital Twin assistant are a combination nobody else replicates. Most people aren't using all of that. They want transcription, summaries, maybe CRM sync, and they don't want a bot in the room.

Char gives you full ownership: open-source, local markdown files, your choice of AI provider, and nothing touching your organization's OAuth or calendar. Jamie packages the bot-free experience with CRM integrations and enterprise compliance, at a higher price point than Read AI Pro. Tactiq is the budget pick at $8/month with real-time transcription and CRM workflows, as long as your meetings happen in the browser.

Granola works well for client-facing calls where the notepad-first approach fits, though the database encryption incident and lifetime meeting cap on free are worth factoring in. Fathom has the best free tier if you're on Zoom, but the bot returns on every other platform. Fireflies covers the deepest CRM automation for sales teams willing to accept a bot, though the BIPA lawsuit adds risk in regulated industries.

Char is free for unlimited local transcription. Download it at [char.com](https://char.com) and try it on your next call. No account required, no calendar permissions, no data leaves your machine unless you want it to.
