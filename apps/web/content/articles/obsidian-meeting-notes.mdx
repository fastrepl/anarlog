You're already in Obsidian. You have a vault with projects, people notes, maybe a daily notes practice. What you don't have is meeting notes that connect to any of it. That's the specific problem this guide solves: building a meeting notes system inside an existing Obsidian PKB, where notes actually link to projects and people.

## What we're building

By the end of this guide you'll have:

- A meeting note template that fills in the date and title, with sections for decisions and action items
- A person note that links back to meeting notes where you linked that colleague
- Action items you can find across your entire vault in one search
- A Markdown handoff from Anarlog to your vault through manual export or an enabled Pro automation

The [Anarlog setup below](#step-8-add-anarlog-for-real-time-transcription-and-ai-notes) explains the export choices and separate transcription and summary providers. Keeping both AI steps on-device requires supported local models; choosing a cloud provider sends the required audio or text to that service.

## How to Take Meeting Notes in Obsidian?

### Step 1: Create the folder structure

![Obsidian vault with Meetings and People folders](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-1-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

Open your vault. Create two folders:

```
Meetings/
People/
```

That's it. You don't need subfolders by month or year yet. If you end up with hundreds of meeting notes and want to organize by date later, Obsidian's search doesn't care about folder depth, it searches everything. Start flat.

If you already have an established vault, just add these two folders alongside whatever you have.

### Step 2: Create a person note

![Person note in Obsidian with role, company, and tags properties](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-2-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

Before you touch templates or meetings, create your first person note. In the `People/` folder, make a new note. Name it after someone you meet with regularly. Something like `Sarah Chen.md`.

Add this to the note:

```
---
role: Engineering Lead
company: Acme
tags:
  - person
---
## About
Engineering lead on the payments team. Reports to VP Eng.
## Meeting history
```

That's a person note. The role and company properties are there so you can search for them later `— [company:Acme]` finds everyone at Acme. The `## Meeting history` section is going to fill itself. We'll get to that.

Make a few of these for people you meet with often. They don't need to be detailed. A name and a role is enough to start.

### Step 3: Build the meeting note template

In your vault, create a `Templates/` folder if you don't have one. Inside it, create a file called `Meeting.md`:

```
---
date: "{{date:YYYY-MM-DD}}"
type: meeting
attendees:
tags:
  - meeting
---
# {{title}}
**Attendees:**
## Notes
## Decisions
## Action items
- [ ]
```

Enable Obsidian's [Templates core plugin](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/templates), then set **Settings → Core plugins → Templates → Template folder location** to `Templates/`.

When you want to start a meeting note: create a new note in `Meetings/`, name it something like `2026-04-06 Product Sync`, place your cursor where you want the template, then use the command palette's **Insert template** command and select `Meeting`. The core plugin inserts the template at that position and replaces the date and title variables. Move to the Notes section to start typing.

### Step 4: Fill in a real meeting note

Here's what a meeting note looks like after a meeting. The key habit: write names and topics as `[[wikilinks]]`.

```
---
date: "2026-04-06"
type: meeting
attendees:
  - "[[Sarah Chen]]"
  - "[[James Park]]"
tags:
  - meeting
  - payments
---
# 2026-04-06 Sprint Planning
**Attendees:** [[Sarah Chen]], [[James Park]]
## Notes
- [[Sarah Chen]] walked through the payments migration timeline
- Target is end of Q2, but dependent on the [[PCI audit]] completing first
- [[James Park]] flagged that the staging environment won't be ready until May 15
- Need to decide if we push the launch or run a parallel environment
## Decisions
- We're keeping the Q2 target but building a fallback plan for parallel environments
- [[Sarah Chen]] owns the fallback proposal, due by Friday
## Action items
- [ ] [[Sarah Chen]] — Write up the parallel environment fallback plan by April 11
- [ ] [[James Park]] — Get staging timeline confirmed with infra team by April 9
- [ ] Follow up with [[Legal]] on PCI audit status
```

Look at what just happened:

- [[Sarah Chen]] appears in multiple places. Her person note now shows this meeting in backlinks — with the surrounding context of what she said, what she decided, and what she owes.
- [[PCI audit]] links to a topic note. If that note doesn't exist yet, that's fine. When the PCI audit becomes important enough to have its own page, every meeting that mentioned it is already connected.
- The action items use - [ ] task syntax. Obsidian's search can find these directly, and you can narrow it to a specific person.

### Step 5: Person notes fill themselves

![Person note with backlinks panel showing linked meeting notes](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-3-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

Open `Sarah Chen.md` in `People/`. Click the backlinks icon in the right sidebar. Every meeting where you wrote `[[Sarah Chen]]` shows up with surrounding context. You can see what was discussed, what she decided, what she owes — all without maintaining anything.

This is the approach from the [Managing notes for meetings thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/managing-notes-for-meetings-and-one-on-ones-effectively/32049) that resonated with the most people. No plugins. No queries. Just links and backlinks doing the work.

If you want a structured table view instead of backlinks, use a Base. The [Automate Base Fill-in](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/automate-base-fill-in-for-meeting-notes/103433) thread shows the exact filter:

```
filters:
  and:
    - file.hasLink(this.file.name)
    - 'type = "meeting"'
```

Embed this Base in Sarah's person note and it renders a table of every meeting note that links to her, filterable and sortable. This uses Obsidian's built-in Bases feature — no community plugins needed.

### Step 6: Create meeting notes fast from your daily note

![Daily note with wikilinks to today's meeting notes](anarlog/blog/library/products/obsidian/daily-notes/2026-04-08-meeting-links.png "char-editor-width=80")

If you use Obsidian's Daily Notes plugin, you probably want to create meeting notes from there. The [Daily Note to Meeting Note thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/from-daily-note-create-a-new-note-with-a-template/87160) shows how to set up a one-click button using Meta Bind and Templater that:

1. Creates a new note in your `Meetings/` folder
2. Applies your meeting template
3. Renames it with today's date
4. Opens it in a new tab

Your daily note becomes a launchpad. Start of the day, you see your calendar, and for each meeting you click a button and you're ready.

If that's too much setup, just do it manually: Cmd+N, type the name, insert template. It takes five seconds.

### Step 7: Finding things later

This is where the system pays off. Every query below has been tested in a live Obsidian vault.

Sarah's open action items:

![search results for open tasks in obsidian](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-5-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

```
task-todo:"Sarah Chen"
```

Finds every incomplete task across your vault that mentions Sarah by name. Returns the exact task text with context.

All meetings tagged payments:

![search filtering in obsidian](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-6-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

```
path:Meetings [tags:payments]
```

Finds meeting notes where the tags property includes "payments." Returns only files inside the Meetings folder.

Every meeting where PCI audit came up:

![Obsidian search for a topic across all meeting notes](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-7-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

```
path:Meetings "PCI audit"
```

Finds every mention of "PCI audit" across all meeting notes, with surrounding context so you can see what was said about it.

All March meetings:

![Obsidian search filtering notes by date property](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-8-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

```
[date:/2026-03/]
```

Uses regex on the date property to find all notes with a March 2026 date. Works for any month — change the pattern to match what you need.

Everyone at a specific company:

![Obsidian property search across person notes](anarlog/blog/articles/obsidian-meeting-notes/image-9-1.png "char-editor-width=80")

*Captured in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), the app used in this workflow.*

```
[company:Acme]
```

Searches the `company` property across person notes. Useful when you're preparing for a client meeting and want to see all your contacts there.

### Step 8: Add Anarlog for real-time transcription and AI notes

Your template captures what you type. A transcript can help you check the decision, name, or deadline you missed while listening. Anarlog, the open-source meeting notetaker we build, can capture microphone and system audio without adding a meeting bot. Tell participants when you record and follow the meeting's consent requirements.

Anarlog stores the original meeting session, notes, and transcript in local SQLite, with attachments kept as separate local files. Markdown is an export format. Setting a storage location alone does not put readable notes in an Obsidian vault.

![Current Anarlog meeting summary view](anarlog/blog/library/products/anarlog/desktop/2026-08-20-meeting-summary.jpg "char-editor-width=80")

Start with a manual export:

1. Finish the meeting and generate a summary if you want one included. Review the transcript and summary for names, decisions, and deadlines.
2. Open the meeting's **Export** dialog in Anarlog. Choose **Markdown** and select the notes, summary, and transcript sections you need.
3. Save the `.md` file in your vault's `Meetings/` folder, or move the exported file there. Open it in Obsidian and link it from your meeting note.

For repeated exports, **Pro Automations** can write Markdown to a chosen folder. In **Settings → Automations**, create a workflow with **After the meeting summary is ready** as its trigger, add **Markdown export**, choose the content sections and your vault's `Meetings/` directory, then select **Save & enable**. Run a short sample and check the resulting file before depending on it for a real meeting. A meeting-end trigger runs at a different stage; do not assume a generated summary is already available then.

This is a one-way export. Editing the vault copy does not update the original session in Anarlog. Keep Anarlog's original data and backups when you need the recording or the complete meeting record. Do not assume an exported file matches the YAML properties or template fields used earlier in this guide; check it and add the properties your Obsidian workflow needs.

Choose transcription and summary providers separately. Local speech models handle transcription; Ollama or LM Studio can handle local summaries. Selecting only a local summary model does not make cloud transcription local. Managed or bring-your-own-key cloud providers receive the audio or text needed for their requests. Follow the [offline transcription setup and checks](/blog/offline-meeting-transcription-mac/) if you need an on-device workflow.

For a broader comparison of editable files and database-backed notes, see the [Markdown note-taking apps guide](/blog/markdown-note-taking-apps/).

## Your Obsidian Meeting Notes Workflow

Monday morning. You open Obsidian.

1. You check your calendar and see three meetings today. From your daily note, you create a meeting note for the first one: `2026-04-07 Sprint Planning`.
2. With participants informed, you start recording in Anarlog. You take notes in Obsidian: quick bullets, [[wikilinks]] for people and topics as they come up.
3. The meeting ends. You stop Anarlog, generate and review the summary, then export Markdown to your vault or check the file from your enabled summary-ready automation. You pull the verified decisions and action items to the top of your meeting note.
4. Before your 1:1 with Sarah that afternoon, you open [[Sarah Chen]] and glance at backlinks to the meeting notes where you linked her. Search `task-todo:"Sarah Chen"` to find her incomplete tasks; in this example, there are two from last week.
5. In the 1:1, you return to the sprint planning note and check the relevant transcript passage. Verify important wording against the authorized recording when available; transcription can contain errors.

That's the system. Templates give you structure. Links connect everything. Backlinks surface history. Search finds anything. Anarlog fills the gap between what you typed and what was actually said. [Download Anarlog now](https://anarlog.so/download/apple-silicon) and try it on your next meeting.
